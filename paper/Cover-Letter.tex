Dear Editor,

I am submitting our manuscript, ``Revisiting Student Sectioning via Node2Vec: A Graph Representation Learning Framework'' for consideration for publication in the \textit{Progress in Artificial Intelligence}.

Student sectioning is a classical problem in university course timetabling. We investigate whether the relational structure implicit in student--course enrollments can provide a more informative representation for student grouping. We construct a weighted student co-enrollment graph and apply Node2Vec to learn low-dimensional student representations. The learned representations are then supplied to standard clustering algorithms without modifying the clustering stage.

A central aspect of the study is a controlled comparison with both conventional representation and alternative graph/dimensionality-reduction approaches. On six real-world university courses involving 341 students, Node2Vec achieves an average Silhouette Score of 0.629 with the neutral configuration ($p{=}1$, $q{=}1$), compared with 0.466 for PCA+KMeans, 0.157 for the conventional enrollment representation, and 0.128 for spectral clustering. These results indicate that graph representation learning provides an improvement beyond the benefit of dimensionality reduction alone.

The manuscript further evaluates multiple clustering algorithms, Node2Vec parameters, clustering stability, practical significance through effect sizes and consistency, additional clustering metrics, visualization, and computational cost. The proposed pipeline remains computationally lightweight for the datasets considered.

The work is closely related to recent graph-learning research published in the journal, including graph convolution networks for clustering \cite{AlJreidy2024Clustering}, graph prototypical networks with active learning \cite{Solgi2022GraphPrototypical}, and link prediction using local and global structure \cite{Luo2022LinkPrediction}. Our study complements these directions by investigating graph representation learning in an educational data-mining application and by examining whether learned graph representations can improve a classical clustering-based problem.

We believe the manuscript fits the journal's scope at the intersection of graph learning, representation learning, clustering, and applied machine learning in education. The work aligns with the journal's focus on machine learning algorithms and techniques, as well as AI applications in education.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. 
The source code and datasets used in this paper are available as supplementary material for reviewers.
Source code and experimental materials are available at
\url{https://github.com/mamintoosi/Node2Vec-SSP}.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
