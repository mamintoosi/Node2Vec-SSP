Student sectioning is a fundamental subproblem of university course timetabling that aims to partition students into balanced sections while preserving scheduling flexibility and efficient resource utilization. Classical clustering-based approaches typically represent students using binary student--course enrollment matrices. Although such representations encode direct enrollment relationships, they may not adequately capture higher-order relationships induced by shared course participation.

This paper revisits student sectioning from the perspective of graph representation learning. We construct a weighted student co-enrollment graph and employ DeepWalk to learn low-dimensional student embeddings from random-walk contexts. The learned representations are then provided to standard clustering algorithms for section formation. To distinguish the effect of graph-based representation learning from dimensionality reduction and direct graph clustering, we compare the proposed approach with the conventional enrollment representation, a PCA-based dimensionality-reduction baseline, and spectral clustering on the co-enrollment graph.

Experiments on six real-world university courses involving 341 students enrolled in 38 courses show that the proposed approach achieves an average Silhouette Score of 0.579, compared with 0.153 for the conventional enrollment representation and 0.460 for PCA followed by KMeans. Spectral clustering achieves an average score of 0.151. The proposed approach provides the strongest overall performance across the six courses, although PCA performs better for one course. The results therefore show that dimensionality reduction accounts for a substantial part of the improvement over the conventional representation, while graph representation learning provides an additional improvement beyond PCA. Additional experiments on clustering algorithms, parameter sensitivity, clustering stability, statistical significance, and runtime further characterize the behavior of the proposed framework.

These results indicate that representing student relationships through a co-enrollment graph can provide useful structural information beyond conventional feature-space dimensionality reduction. The study demonstrates how graph representation learning can be incorporated into existing student sectioning pipelines without modifying the downstream clustering algorithms, providing a simple approach for improving student groupings used in subsequent timetabling processes
